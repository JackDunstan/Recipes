# Red-Cooked Pork Steak Braise (Hong Shao Style)

Thin pork steaks braised in a glossy soy-sugar-Shaoxing sauce with warm spices. Adapted from classic hong shao rou for leaner cuts — shorter, gentler braise so the meat goes silky rather than stringy. Freezes exceptionally well; the sauce improves after a freeze-thaw.

## Ingredients

- 1200 g thin pork steaks, cut into 4–5 cm wide strips across the grain
- 30 ml neutral oil
- 40 g rock sugar (or white sugar)
- 80 ml Shaoxing wine
- 45 ml light soy sauce
- 20 ml dark soy sauce
- 400 ml water (or unsalted chicken stock)
- 30 g ginger, sliced into coins
- 15 g garlic cloves, lightly smashed
- 30 g spring onions, cut into 5 cm lengths
- 2 whole star anise
- ½ cinnamon quill (~3 cm) — *or* a third star anise plus ¼ tsp (0.5 g) five spice
- 2 dried red chillies (optional)

## Directions

1. Preheat oven to 150°C (fan 140°C). Pat pork very dry and cut into 4–5 cm wide strips across the grain.
2. Heat oil in the Dutch oven over high heat. Sear pork in 2–3 batches until well browned on both sides, about 2 minutes per side. Don't crowd the pot. Set aside.
3. Reduce heat to medium-low. Add sugar to the pork fat and let it melt and darken to a deep amber — watch closely, it turns fast.
4. Immediately add Shaoxing wine (it will spit), then both soy sauces and the water. Scrape up the fond. Add ginger, garlic, spring onions, star anise, cinnamon, and chillies.
5. Return the pork and any juices. Liquid should come about two-thirds up the meat. Bring to a simmer, cover, and transfer to the oven for 1 hour 30 minutes, until fork-tender.
6. Remove pork to a plate. Fish out the aromatics and whole spices. Simmer the sauce on the stovetop over medium-high heat until glossy and reduced by about half — it should coat a spoon.
7. Return the pork, turn to coat in the glaze, and warm through for a couple of minutes. Serve over steamed rice with blanched greens.

## Notes

- Steaks are much leaner than belly — don't extend the braise beyond fork-tender or they'll dry out.
- Rock sugar gives the classic glassy sheen. Available at New Asia Supermarket, Russell St.
- If freezing, slightly under-reduce the sauce (step 6) so it doesn't over-concentrate on reheating.
- Australian supermarket "cinnamon" quills are usually cassia anyway, just milder in stick form — a fine substitute.

## Storage

Cool completely, portion with plenty of sauce covering the meat. Freeze up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat gently on the stove. Add fresh greens at serving time, not before freezing. Fridge: 4 days.
