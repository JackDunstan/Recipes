# Chongqing Spicy Beef Noodle Soup (Hi CQ-style)

## Ingredients

- 400g beef shank (gravy beef)
- 300g beef tendon
- 800g beef bones (for stock)
- 400g thin ramen or alkaline noodles (fresh or dried)
- 8 dried red chillies
- 1.5 tbsp Sichuan peppercorns (whole)
- 2 tbsp doubanjiang (chilli bean paste)
- 2 tbsp chilli oil / la jiao you
- 1 tsp five-spice powder
- 3 star anise
- 1 cinnamon stick
- 6 garlic cloves, smashed
- 30g fresh ginger, sliced
- 1 brown onion, roughly chopped
- 3 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp dark soy sauce
- 3 tbsp Shaoxing rice wine
- 15g rock sugar (or brown sugar)
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 4 spring onions, sliced
- 1 cup fresh coriander
- 3 tbsp roasted peanuts, roughly crushed
- 2 bok choy or Chinese broccoli
- 2.5L water

## Directions

1. Place beef shank and beef tendon in a large pot with cold water. Bring to a boil and cook for 5 minutes. Drain and rinse under cold water to remove impurities. Set aside.
2. In a clean large pot, add beef bones and 2.5L water. Bring to a boil, skim foam, then reduce to a simmer. Add 3 star anise, 1 cinnamon stick, half the garlic, half the ginger, and the onion. Simmer uncovered for at least 1.5 hours to build a rich bone broth.
3. In a separate large pot or Dutch oven, heat neutral oil over medium heat. Add remaining garlic, ginger, and onion. Fry for 1–2 minutes until fragrant. Add doubanjiang and stir for another minute. Add dried red chillies and Sichuan peppercorns and toast briefly — 30 seconds.
4. Add the blanched beef shank and tendon to the spice pot. Pour in Shaoxing rice wine and let it sizzle. Add light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, rock sugar, five-spice powder, and strain in enough bone stock to fully submerge the beef (around 1.5–2L). Bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer.
5. Cover and braise on low heat. The beef shank will be done after about 1.5 hours — remove and set aside. Continue cooking the tendon for a further 30–60 minutes until very gelatinous and yields easily when pressed — it should wobble, not bounce. Remove and rest.
6. Remove solids from the braising liquid and strain the broth through a fine sieve. Taste and adjust — add soy for salt, chilli oil for heat, and more bone stock to reach your desired concentration. Keep on low heat. Slice the beef shank and tendon into thick pieces once cooled slightly.
7. Cook noodles according to packet instructions (fresh ramen noodles typically take 1–2 minutes). In the same water, quickly blanch bok choy or Chinese broccoli for 30 seconds. Drain both well.
8. Place noodles in a deep bowl. Ladle over generous hot broth. Arrange sliced beef and tendon on top. Add blanched greens. Finish with sliced spring onions, fresh coriander, crushed peanuts, and a drizzle of extra chilli oil. Serve immediately.

## Notes

- Tendon texture is everything — it should be gelatinous and unctuous (collagen, not fat). Don't rush the braise.
- Sichuan peppercorns give the signature numbing (má) sensation — toast them lightly for best flavour.
- Use pixian doubanjiang if available — it gives the best depth of chilli flavour. Available at New Asia Supermarket (Russell St) or Minh Phat (Footscray).
- Noodle type matters — use thin ramen-style alkaline noodles, not udon or rice noodles. Fresh from Asian supermarkets is ideal.
- Beef oil: render some beef fat or suet in a pan until golden, strain, and drizzle a spoonful over the finished bowl for extra richness.
- Make the broth a day ahead — it tastes significantly better the next day.

## Storage

Broth and braised beef keep refrigerated for up to 4 days or frozen for 3 months. Store separately from noodles and toppings. Reheat broth gently on the stovetop.
