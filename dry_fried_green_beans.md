# Dry-Fried Green Beans with Crispy Pork (干煸四季豆)

## Ingredients

- 500g green beans, topped and tailed
- 250g pork mince
- 22g doubanjiang (chilli bean paste)
- 20g garlic, minced
- 10g fresh ginger, minced
- 15ml Shaoxing wine
- 5ml soy sauce
- 2g sugar
- 5ml sesame oil
- 45ml neutral oil (e.g. vegetable)
- 2g dried chillies, whole (optional)

## Directions

1. Wash the green beans and dry them thoroughly — any water will cause violent spitting in the wok. Snap into 6–7cm lengths.
2. Heat 45ml neutral oil in a wok over the highest heat you have until just smoking. Add the beans in a single layer — don't touch them for 30 seconds, then toss. Keep cooking, tossing occasionally, until the skins are wrinkled and blistered with some charred spots. This takes 6–8 minutes. Remove and set aside.
3. Add a small splash more oil if needed. Add the pork mince and spread it flat. Don't stir — let it fry undisturbed until the bottom is golden and crispy, 2–3 minutes. Break it up, then fry again until genuinely crunchy and deeply golden throughout. Go longer than feels comfortable.
4. Push the pork to the side. Add the doubanjiang to the cleared space and fry for 30 seconds until the oil turns red and fragrant. Add the garlic, ginger, and dried chillies if using, and fry for another 30 seconds.
5. Return the beans to the wok. Add the Shaoxing wine, soy sauce, and sugar. Toss everything together over high heat for 1–2 minutes until the beans are well coated and any remaining liquid is gone — it should look dry and clingy, not saucy. Finish with sesame oil, toss once more, and serve immediately.

## Notes

- Doubanjiang: Pixian doubanjiang (郫县豆瓣) is the gold standard — look for it at New Asia Supermarket on Russell St or Minh Phat in Footscray. It's chunkier and deeper than generic chilli bean pastes. If you can only find Lee Kum Kee, use slightly less as it's saltier.
- Wok vs cast iron: If using cast iron, crank heat early and be patient — it holds heat well but takes longer to get there. The beans may take a minute or two longer to blister properly.
- The mince: A slightly higher fat pork mince (not lean) crisps better. Butcher mince is ideal.
- Beans: Chinese restaurants often use snake beans (长豆角). If you can find them at QVM or an Asian grocer, they give a more authentic result — just cut into 6–7cm lengths.
- Restaurant shortcut: Many restaurants deep-fry the beans at 190°C for 60–90 seconds instead of dry-frying. Messier but gives a more uniform blister.
