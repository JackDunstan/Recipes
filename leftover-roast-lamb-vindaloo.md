# Leftover Roast Lamb Vindaloo (Bird's Eye)

## Ingredients

- 450 g cooked roast lamb, cut into 3 cm chunks
- 10 g fresh bird's eye chillies, stems removed
- 10 g sweet paprika (for colour, optional)
- 25 g garlic, peeled
- 25 g fresh ginger, peeled
- 60 ml malt or white vinegar
- 4 g cumin seeds
- 3 g coriander seeds
- 3 g black peppercorns
- 1 g whole cloves
- 3 g cinnamon stick
- 2 g ground turmeric
- 60 ml neutral oil
- 200 g brown onion, finely sliced
- 10 g jaggery or brown sugar
- 5 g fine salt
- 300 ml water or lamb stock

## Directions

1. In a dry cast iron pan over medium-low heat, toast the cumin seeds, coriander seeds, peppercorns, cloves and cinnamon for 60–90 seconds until fragrant. Pull them the moment they smell toasty — the pan's residual heat will keep them cooking.
2. Blend the bird's eye chillies, toasted spices, garlic, ginger, turmeric, paprika and vinegar into a smooth paste. Add a splash of water if needed to get it moving.
3. Heat the oil in the cast iron over medium heat and fry the onion until deep golden, 10–12 minutes. Don't rush this — it's the body of the sauce.
4. Add the paste and fry, stirring often, for 5–6 minutes until it darkens and the oil starts to separate at the edges. Drop the heat if it catches. Get the rangehood on — fresh chilli fumes are punchy.
5. Stir in the water or stock, jaggery and salt. Bring to a simmer and cook uncovered for 5 minutes.
6. Add the lamb, coat well, cover and simmer gently for 35–40 minutes so it absorbs the sauce. Stir occasionally and add splashes of water if it thickens too much. Lowest flame on cast iron.
7. Rest off the heat for 10 minutes. Taste and adjust — more vinegar for sharpness or a pinch more sugar to round it out. Serve with steamed rice.

## Notes

- Hot — fresh bird's eye chillies in place of the traditional dried Kashmiris; the paprika replaces the colour and body Kashmiris would provide without adding heat. Traditional Goan vindaloo (from Portuguese carne de vinha d'alhos) uses no tomato — the tang comes entirely from vinegar. Goan versions use coconut or palm vinegar; malt vinegar is the common substitute. Since the lamb is already cooked, keep the simmer gentle — a hard boil will tighten and dry it out. Like most vindaloos, it's better the next day.

## Storage

Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 4 days. Freezes well for up to 3 months; thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat gently with a splash of water.
