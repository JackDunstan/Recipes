# Tuna Mornay Bake

## Ingredients

- 160 g dried macaroni (or elbow/penne)
- 425 g tinned tuna in springwater, drained (1 x 425g tin)
- 420 g condensed cream of mushroom soup (1 tin)
- 100 ml milk
- 100 g frozen peas
- 80 g brown onion, finely diced
- 20 g butter
- 10 g Dijon mustard
- 20 ml lemon juice
- 100 g tasty cheese, grated
- 30 g panko breadcrumbs
- 5 ml Worcestershire sauce
- 5 g flat-leaf parsley, chopped
- Black pepper, to taste

## Directions

1. Heat oven to 200C (180C fan). Boil the macaroni in well-salted water to just under al dente - it keeps cooking in the oven. Drain and set aside.
2. Melt the butter in a pan over medium heat and cook the onion with a pinch of salt until soft and translucent, no colour.
3. Turn the heat to low. Stir in the condensed soup and loosen with the milk until smooth and just pourable. Add the Dijon, Worcestershire and about 30 g of the grated cheese; stir until the cheese melts.
4. Fold through the tuna, peas, parsley and lemon juice. Season with black pepper - taste before adding salt, as the soup is already salty.
5. Fold the drained pasta through the sauce until well coated, then tip into a baking dish.
6. Scatter over the remaining cheese and the panko breadcrumbs.
7. Bake for about 20 minutes, until bubbling at the edges and golden on top.
8. Rest a few minutes so the sauce sets slightly before serving.

## Notes

- The 70s/80s weeknight classic. Period-correct lifts that don't break the nostalgia: the Dijon, Worcestershire and lemon cut through the richness of the condensed soup; leave them out for the pure pantry-bowl version. The whole tin of condensed soup with only 100 ml milk is deliberate (the soup is the thickener, so no roux) - take the milk to 150 ml for a looser sauce. Panko top is optional but gives a crisp lid; crushed cornflakes is the truly old-school move. Tuna in springwater keeps it clean; if using tuna in oil, drain well or the bake turns greasy.

## Storage

Keeps in the fridge 3 days in an airtight container and reheats well (covered, 180C, or microwave). Freezes for up to 2 months; thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.
