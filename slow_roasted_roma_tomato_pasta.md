# Slow-Roasted Roma Tomato Pasta

## Ingredients

- 800g Roma tomatoes (egg-sized), quartered lengthways, seeds and gel scooped out
- 60ml extra virgin olive oil, plus extra to finish
- 30g garlic cloves, peeled and left whole
- 8g fine salt
- 5g caster sugar
- 2g dried chilli flakes
- 2g freshly cracked black pepper
- 400g dried spaghetti or rigatoni
- 15g fresh basil leaves, torn
- 40g Parmigiano Reggiano, finely grated, plus extra to serve
- Salt for the pasta water (approx. 10g per litre)

## Directions

1. Heat the oven to 160C (fan-forced 145C).
2. Quarter the tomatoes lengthways. Run a thumb through each quarter to scoop out the seeds and watery gel, and discard.
3. Put the tomatoes into a cast iron pan, Dutch oven or roasting tray, ideally in a single layer, cut sides facing up where possible. Tuck the whole garlic cloves in among them.
4. Pour over the olive oil and scatter with the salt, sugar, chilli flakes and black pepper. Toss gently with your hands to coat, then settle the tomatoes back into a single layer.
5. Roast for 60 to 75 minutes, until the tomatoes have collapsed, wrinkled and caramelised at the edges and the oil around them is deeply coloured. Do not stir during roasting.
6. In the last 15 minutes, bring a large pot of well-salted water to a rolling boil and cook the pasta until just short of al dente, about 1 minute less than the packet time.
7. Remove the tomatoes from the oven. Crush the softened garlic cloves against the side of the pan with a fork and lightly crush about half the tomatoes, leaving the rest in pieces for texture.
8. Reserve 250ml of the pasta water, then drain the pasta.
9. Tip the pasta directly into the pan of tomatoes, add 100ml of the pasta water and the grated Parmigiano, and toss vigorously over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes until the sauce emulsifies and clings to the pasta. Add more pasta water a splash at a time if it looks tight.
10. Take off the heat, fold through the torn basil and taste for salt.
11. Serve immediately with a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil and more grated Parmigiano.

## Notes

- Egg-sized Romas hold more moisture and gel than smaller ones. Quartering rather than halving exposes more surface area so they concentrate instead of steaming, and scooping the seeds out gives a richer, clingier sauce.
- Moderate-flavoured tomatoes benefit enormously from the long, low roast. The sugar is not for sweetness so much as to help caramelisation and round out any acidity.
- Roast in a cast iron pan or Dutch oven if you have one. The even heat retention gives better browning at the edges. Keep the oven at 160C rather than going hotter, or the garlic will scorch before the tomatoes collapse.
- Go by look, not the clock. The tomatoes are ready when they have slumped, wrinkled and started to caramelise at the edges, and the oil has taken on their colour.
- The skins can be left on for a rustic, confit-style sauce. If you want it smoother, they slip off easily once roasted.
- For a heartier version, add 50g of anchovy fillets or a spoon of tomato paste to the pan for the last 20 minutes of roasting.

## Storage

The roasted tomatoes and their oil keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days, or frozen for 3 months. Dressed pasta is best eaten immediately. Reheat leftover sauce gently with a splash of water to loosen.
