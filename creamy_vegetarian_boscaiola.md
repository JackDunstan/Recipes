# Creamy Vegetarian Boscaiola

## Ingredients

- 400g dried pasta (rigatoni, pappardelle, or fettuccine)
- 500g mixed mushrooms (Swiss brown, button, or a mix with a few dried porcini)
- 15g dried porcini mushrooms (optional but highly recommended)
- 3 shallots, finely diced
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 120ml dry white wine
- 300ml thickened cream
- 120g frozen peas
- 60g parmesan, finely grated
- 30g unsalted butter
- 30ml olive oil
- 4 fresh thyme sprigs
- Salt and black pepper to taste
- 180ml pasta cooking water (reserved)

## Directions

1. Soak dried porcini in 200ml of just-boiled water for 20 minutes. Once soft, lift out and roughly chop. Pour the soaking liquid through a fine sieve or paper towel and reserve.
2. Tear or slice fresh mushrooms into substantial pieces — don't go too small or they'll disappear into the sauce. Pat dry with paper towel.
3. Heat olive oil in a wide, heavy pan over high heat until shimmering. Add mushrooms in a single layer — work in batches if needed. Leave them alone for 2–3 minutes until deeply golden on one side, then toss. Season with salt only after they've coloured. Set aside.
4. Reduce heat to medium-low (low if using cast iron). Add butter to the same pan. Add shallots with a pinch of salt and cook gently for 5–6 minutes until very soft and translucent. Add garlic and thyme and cook a further 2 minutes.
5. Pour in white wine and scrape up any fond from the base of the pan. Add the strained porcini soaking liquid. Let it reduce by about two-thirds over medium heat — around 5 minutes.
6. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to the boil and cook pasta to 2 minutes shy of al dente. Reserve 180ml of pasta cooking water before draining.
7. Return all mushrooms (including chopped porcini) to the pan. Pour in cream. Simmer over medium heat for 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce is noticeably thickened and coats the back of a spoon.
8. Add frozen peas directly — they'll heat through in 90 seconds. Add drained pasta and a splash of pasta cooking water. Toss vigorously over medium heat for 2 minutes, adding more pasta water as needed to keep the sauce silky. Remove thyme stalks.
9. Off the heat, stir through parmesan. Taste and adjust salt and pepper. Serve immediately with extra parmesan and cracked black pepper.

## Notes

- Mushroom sourcing: Swiss brown mushrooms from a good greengrocer or deli are far better than supermarket buttons — they hold up to heat and have more flavour. If you can find fresh porcini or slippery jacks at a farmers market, even better. The dried porcini soak is the biggest single flavour upgrade you can make. Pasta shape: Wide, ridged shapes catch the creamy sauce best. Rigatoni or pappardelle are ideal. Avoid anything too thin. Make it richer: A tablespoon of mascarpone stirred in at the end instead of (or alongside) some of the parmesan gives an even creamier, more unctuous result — closer to what you'd get in a fresh market sauce. Cast iron note: Drop the shallot step to low rather than medium-low to avoid scorching; the cream reduction is fine on cast iron at a gentle simmer.

## Storage

This sauce does not store well — cream sauces split on reheating unless you add a splash of milk and reheat very low and slow. Best made and served fresh.
