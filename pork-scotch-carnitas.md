# Pork Scotch Carnitas

## Ingredients

- Pork scotch fillet, cut into ~5cm chunks — 600g
- Lard or neutral oil — 30g
- Brown onion, halved — 110g
- Garlic, smashed — 16g
- Orange juice (plus the spent halves) — 80ml
- Lime juice — 15ml
- Ground cumin — 4g
- Dried oregano (Mexican if available) — 1.5g
- Fine salt — 9g
- Ground black pepper — 2g
- Bay leaves — 2
- Cinnamon stick, small piece — 2g
- Water — 250ml

## Directions

1. Season. Pat the pork dry, toss with cumin, oregano, salt and pepper. Heat oven to 150°C.
2. Brown. Melt the lard in the cast iron Dutch oven over medium, letting it come up gradually. Brown the pork in two batches, ~3 min a side, without crowding. Set aside.
3. Build the braise. Return all pork. Add onion, garlic, orange juice (squeeze then drop the halves in), lime juice, bay, cinnamon and water. Liquid should reach about halfway up. Bring to a gentle simmer.
4. Slow braise. Lid on, into the oven ~2.5 hours, turning once, until fork-tender.
5. Shred & reduce. Lift pork out, shred with two forks. Discard orange halves, bay and cinnamon. Reduce the liquid on the stovetop to a few tablespoons of glossy fat and juice.
6. Crisp. Toss pork through the reduced fat, spread on a tray, grill on high until edges caramelise — crisp tips, not dry. Finish with lime.

## Notes

- Not sweet by design — the orange is for fragrance and acidity, not sugar. For even less sweetness, drop orange to 60ml and lean on the lime.
- Salt at 9g is ~1.5% of the pork weight (textbook seasoning). Most stays in the dish since the braise reduces back into the meat. Start at 7g and taste before crisping if holding back.
- Cast iron retains heat hard — keep the flame low when reducing the liquid or it'll catch. Do the final crisp on a separate tray under the grill, not in the pot.
- Mexican oregano is more citrusy and less medicinal than Mediterranean; standard dried oregano is fine.
- Serve on warm corn tortillas with white onion, coriander and lime.

## Storage

Keeps 3 days in the fridge. The fat helps it reheat well.
